Topic 1 — Partial Derivatives

Question 1

Let \( f(x,y)=x^3y^2+2x^2y-5y^3 \).

Tasks

  1. Find \(f_x\) and \(f_y\).
  2. Evaluate \(f_x(1,-1)\) and \(f_y(1,-1)\).
  3. Interpret the meaning of these values as rates of change.

Question 2

Let \( f(x,y)=\dfrac{x^2+y}{x-y} \).

Tasks

  1. Find the first partial derivatives.
  2. State where these derivatives are defined.
  3. Determine whether \(f_x = f_y\) at \((1,0)\).

Question 3

Let \( z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+4} \).

Tasks

  1. Compute \(z_x\) and \(z_y\).
  2. Find \(z_x(0,2)\) and \(z_y(0,2)\).
  3. State whether the function is differentiable everywhere in its domain.

Question 4

Let \( f(x,y,z)=x^2yz+3xyz^2-z^3 \).

Tasks

  1. Find \(f_x,\; f_y,\; f_z\).
  2. Evaluate all three at \((1,-1,2)\).
  3. Identify which variable has the largest instantaneous effect at that point.

Question 5

Let \( f(x,y)=x^2y^3+e^{xy} \).

Tasks

  1. Find \(f_{xx},\; f_{xy},\; f_{yx},\; f_{yy}\).
  2. Verify whether \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\).
  3. State the theorem that justifies your answer.

Question 6

Given \( x^2+y^2+z^2=9 \).

Tasks

  1. Use implicit differentiation to find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\).
  2. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial y}\).
  3. Evaluate both at the point \((1,2,2)\).

Question 7

Given \( x^2+xy+y^2=7 \).

Tasks

  1. Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) by implicit differentiation.
  2. Evaluate \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) at \((2,1)\).
  3. Determine the slope of the tangent line at that point.

Question 8

Let \( f(x,y)=x^2y+3xy^2 \).

Tasks

  1. Find the differential \(df\).
  2. Use it to estimate the change in \(f\) when \(x\) changes from \(1\) to \(1.03\) and \(y\) changes from \(2\) to \(1.98\).
  3. State whether this is an exact change or an approximation.

Question 9

Let \( f(x,y,z)=\ln(x+y+z) \).

Tasks

  1. Find the first-order partial derivatives with respect to \(x,y,z\).
  2. Find the second-order partial derivatives \(f_{xx},\;f_{yy},\;f_{zz}\).
  3. State the domain of the function.

Question 10

The volume of a right circular cone is \( V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \).

Tasks

  1. Find the differential \(dV\).
  2. Use differentials to estimate the maximum percentage error in \(V\) if the error in \(r\) is at most \(2\%\) and the error in \(h\) is at most \(3\%\).
  3. State which measurement contributes more strongly to the volume error.

Topic 2 — Chain Rule

Question 1

Let \( z=x^2+y^2,\quad x=t^2,\quad y=2t+1 \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{dz}{dt}\) using the chain rule.
  2. Evaluate at \(t=1\).
  3. Verify your answer by direct substitution.

Question 2

Let \( z=xy+x^2,\quad x=\sin t,\quad y=\cos t \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{dz}{dt}\).
  2. Evaluate at \(t=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\).
  3. Determine whether \(z\) is increasing or decreasing at that value of \(t\).

Question 3

Let \( w=x^2+y^2+z^2,\quad x=t,\; y=t^2,\; z=t^3 \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{dw}{dt}\).
  2. Evaluate at \(t=1\).
  3. Interpret the result.

Question 4

Let \( z=e^{xy},\quad x=u+v,\quad y=u-v \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial u}\).
  2. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial v}\).
  3. Evaluate both at \((u,v)=(1,0)\).

Question 5

Let \( z=\ln(x+y),\quad x=r\cos\theta,\quad y=r\sin\theta \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial r}\).
  2. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial \theta}\).
  3. Simplify your answers fully.

Question 6

Let \( z=x^2y+xy^2,\quad x=s+t,\quad y=s-t \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial s}\).
  2. Find \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial t}\).
  3. Evaluate both at \((s,t)=(1,2)\).

Question 7

Let \( w=xyz,\quad x=u^2,\quad y=u+v,\quad z=v^2 \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial u}\).
  2. Find \(\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial v}\).
  3. Evaluate at \((u,v)=(1,1)\).

Question 8

Suppose \( z=f(x,y),\quad x=r\cos\theta,\quad y=r\sin\theta \).

  1. Write the chain rule formula for \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial r}\).
  2. Write the chain rule formula for \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial \theta}\).
  3. State the meaning of \(f_x\) and \(f_y\) in these formulas.

Question 9

A particle moves according to \( x=t^2+1,\quad y=3t,\quad z=x^2-y \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{dz}{dt}\).
  2. Evaluate at \(t=1\).
  3. State whether \(z\) is increasing or decreasing at that instant.

Question 10

Let \( u=x^2+y^2,\quad x=a+b,\quad y=a-b \).

  1. Find \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial a}\).
  2. Find \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial b}\).
  3. Show that your answer agrees with the expression obtained by substituting first.

Answers — Topic 1: Partial Derivatives

Q1

\( f_x = 3x^2y^2 + 4xy \)

\( f_y = 2x^3y + 2x^2 - 15y^2 \)

At \((1,-1)\):

\( f_x(1,-1) = 3(1)(1) + 4(1)(-1) = -1 \)

\( f_y(1,-1) = 2(1)(-1) + 2(1) - 15(1) = -15 \)

Q2

\( f_x = \dfrac{2x(x-y)-(x^2+y)}{(x-y)^2} \)

\( f_y = \dfrac{(x-y)+(x^2+y)}{(x-y)^2} = \dfrac{x^2+x}{(x-y)^2} \)

Defined for \( x \ne y \).

At \((1,0)\): \( f_x = 1,\; f_y = 2 \). So \( f_x \ne f_y \).

Q3

\( z_x = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+4}}, \quad z_y = \dfrac{y}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+4}} \)

At \((0,2)\): \( z_x = 0, \quad z_y = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{8}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \)

Differentiable everywhere since the denominator is never zero.

Q4

\( f_x = 2xyz + 3yz^2 \)

\( f_y = x^2z + 3xz^2 \)

\( f_z = x^2y + 6xyz - 3z^2 \)

At \((1,-1,2)\): \( f_x = -16,\; f_y = 14,\; f_z = -25 \).

\(z\) has the largest effect since \(|f_z| = 25\) is the greatest.

Q5

\( f_{xx} = 2y^3 + y^2 e^{xy} \)

\( f_{xy} = 6xy^2 + e^{xy} + xye^{xy} \)

\( f_{yx} = 6xy^2 + e^{xy} + xye^{xy} \)

\( f_{yy} = 6x^2y + x^2 e^{xy} \)

\( f_{xy} = f_{yx} \) by the Mixed Derivative Theorem (Clairaut's Theorem), since all partials are continuous.

Q6

\( 2x + 2z\,z_x = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; z_x = -\dfrac{x}{z} \)

\( 2y + 2z\,z_y = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; z_y = -\dfrac{y}{z} \)

At \((1,2,2)\): \( z_x = -\dfrac{1}{2},\; z_y = -1 \).

Q7

\( 2x + y + (x+2y)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \)

\( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2x+y}{x+2y} \)

At \((2,1)\): \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{5}{4} \). The slope is \(-\dfrac{5}{4}\).

Q8

\( f_x = 2xy + 3y^2,\quad f_y = x^2 + 6xy \)

\( df = (2xy+3y^2)\,dx + (x^2+6xy)\,dy \)

At \((1,2)\): \( f_x = 16,\; f_y = 13 \).

With \(dx = 0.03,\; dy = -0.02\):

\( df = 16(0.03) + 13(-0.02) = 0.48 - 0.26 = 0.22 \)

This is an approximation.

Q9

\( f_x = f_y = f_z = \dfrac{1}{x+y+z} \)

\( f_{xx} = f_{yy} = f_{zz} = -\dfrac{1}{(x+y+z)^2} \)

Domain: \( x + y + z > 0 \).

Q10

\( dV = \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r h\,dr + \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2\,dh \)

\( \dfrac{dV}{V} \approx 2\dfrac{dr}{r} + \dfrac{dh}{h} \)

Maximum percentage error: \( 2(2\%) + 3\% = 7\% \).

The radius contributes more because its error is doubled.

Answers — Topic 2: Chain Rule

Q1

\( \dfrac{dz}{dt} = 2x(2t) + 2y(2) = 4t^3 + 8t + 4 \)

At \(t=1\): \( 4+8+4 = 16 \).

Q2

\( \dfrac{dz}{dt} = \sin t(-\sin t) + \cos t(\cos t) + 2\sin t\cos t \)

\( = \cos 2t + \sin 2t \)

At \(t = \pi/4\): \( 0 + 1 = 1 \). Increasing.

Q3

\( \dfrac{dw}{dt} = 2t + 4t^3 + 6t^5 \)

At \(t=1\): \( 12 \).

Q4

\( z_u = (x+y)e^{xy},\quad z_v = (y-x)e^{xy} \)

At \((u,v)=(1,0)\): \(x=1,\;y=1\).

\( z_u = 2e,\quad z_v = 0 \).

Q5

\( z_r = \dfrac{\cos\theta + \sin\theta}{r(\cos\theta + \sin\theta)} = \dfrac{1}{r} \)

\( z_\theta = \dfrac{-\sin\theta + \cos\theta}{\cos\theta + \sin\theta} \)

Q6

Substitute: \( z = 2s^3 - 2st^2 \).

\( z_s = 6s^2 - 2t^2,\quad z_t = -4st \)

At \((1,2)\): \( z_s = 6-8 = -2,\quad z_t = -8 \).

Q7

\( w = u^2(u+v)v^2 \)

\( w_u = v^2(3u^2 + 2uv),\quad w_v = u^2(2uv + 3v^2) \)

At \((1,1)\): \( w_u = 5,\quad w_v = 5 \).

Q8

\( \dfrac{\partial z}{\partial r} = f_x\cos\theta + f_y\sin\theta \)

\( \dfrac{\partial z}{\partial \theta} = -f_x\,r\sin\theta + f_y\,r\cos\theta \)

\(f_x\) and \(f_y\) are partial derivatives of \(f\) with respect to its own variables \(x\) and \(y\).

Q9

\( \dfrac{dz}{dt} = 2x(2t) - 3 \)

At \(t=1\): \(x = 2\).

\( \dfrac{dz}{dt} = 2(2)(2) - 3 = 5 \). Increasing.

Q10

\( u_a = 2(x+y) = 4a \)

\( u_b = 2(x-y) = 4b \)

Direct substitution: \( u = (a+b)^2 + (a-b)^2 = 2a^2+2b^2 \).

\( u_a = 4a,\; u_b = 4b \). Agrees. ✓

Answers — Topic 3: Directional Derivatives & Gradient

Q1

\( \nabla f = \langle 2xy,\; x^2+6y \rangle \)

At \((1,2)\): \( \langle 4, 13 \rangle \).

Direction of maximum increase is along \(\langle 4,13\rangle\).

Q2

\( \nabla f(1,1) = \langle 2,2\rangle \).

Unit direction: \( \left\langle \frac{3}{5},\frac{4}{5}\right\rangle \).

\( D_{\mathbf{u}}f = \frac{6}{5} + \frac{8}{5} = \frac{14}{5} \).

Maximum rate: \( 2\sqrt{2} \) in direction \( \left\langle \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle \).

Q3

\( \nabla f(2,1) = \langle 1,2\rangle \).

Greatest decrease: \( \langle -1,-2\rangle \).

Max directional derivative: \( \sqrt{5} \). Min: \( -\sqrt{5} \).

Q4

\( \nabla f(1,1) = \langle 2,-2\rangle \).

Steepest ascent: \( \left\langle \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle \).

Steepest descent: \( \left\langle -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle \).

In direction \(\langle 1,1\rangle\): \( D_{\mathbf{u}}f = 0 \).

Q5

\( \nabla f(1,1,1) = \langle 2,2,2\rangle \).
